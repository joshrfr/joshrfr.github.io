research interests

Next-Generation Communications & Networking

Building better infrastructure for how information moves between systems: wireless and cellular networks, fiber and optical transport, routing and switching, 5G/6G, and programmable, software-defined networks, including SDN and intent-based/intent-defined networking approaches (in the vein of what Meter offers for enterprise networks).

Distributed, Edge & Cloud Systems

Designing and operating infrastructure made of many cooperating components: distributed storage, edge and cloud computing, virtualization, and clustering, with a focus on how compute, storage, and network resources are placed, coordinated, and kept consistent at scale.

Intelligent & Autonomous Networked Systems

Systems that observe their own state and adapt without a human manually driving every decision: autonomous control, optimization, and intelligent resource management, applied directly to real infrastructure rather than treated as a research abstraction.

Applications of AI

Applying machine learning and AI techniques to concrete engineering problems: traffic prediction, anomaly and failure detection, resource scheduling, and decision-making under uncertainty, rather than AI as a standalone research subject.

Resilient Infrastructure & Mission-Critical Communications

Designing systems that keep functioning when things fail: degraded links, constrained bandwidth, intermittent connectivity, and disaster-damaged infrastructure. Failure and uncertainty are treated as the normal operating condition to design for, not the exception.

Space & Non-Terrestrial Networking

Satellite and LEO constellation links, ground-space-ground mesh design, and multi-orbit backhaul as an extension of terrestrial network resilience.

Open Source & Standards

Skepticism of vendor lock-in and closed ecosystems, and a commitment to cross-provider interoperability. This is reflected in open-source infrastructure tooling and in following and participating in standards discussions with organizations including IEEE, IETF, and the O-RAN Alliance.

about

I grew up in Tampa, Florida, and have been interested in technology for as long as I can remember. Some of my earliest interests were computers and aviation, mostly because I was fascinated by complicated systems and wanted to understand how all of the pieces worked together.

My path into technology has been very hands-on. Before I ever thought seriously about research, I was learning by troubleshooting computers, working with networks, supporting real users, and gradually taking on larger infrastructure problems. I eventually began building my own technology business as well, which gave me a different perspective on engineering: a system can be technically interesting, but at the end of the day it still has to work reliably for the people depending on it.

At some point, simply fixing problems after they happened stopped being enough. I wanted to understand why systems failed in the first place, how they could respond better when conditions changed, and how we could design infrastructure that was more intelligent and resilient from the beginning. I also became increasingly interested in designing technology responsibly: building systems that are durable, repairable, efficient, and environmentally conscious rather than treating hardware as disposable or allowing useful technology to unnecessarily end up in landfills. To me, good engineering should consider not only whether something works today, but whether it is ethical, sustainable, maintainable, and built to last.

That curiosity is what pulled me deeper into research at FAMU and into the work I am doing today. What I enjoy most is working somewhere between research and engineering. I like taking an idea, questioning it, building something around it, and then seeing what happens when it meets real constraints. I am naturally curious and tend to follow interesting problems across boundaries rather than staying inside one narrow area, which is probably why my work has gradually brought together communications, computing, infrastructure, and intelligent systems.

I'm still early in that journey. As I finish my undergraduate degree, I want to continue growing as an engineer and researcher, contribute to work that has practical value, and eventually pursue graduate study. More than anything, I want to keep learning, building, and working on difficult problems with people who are equally curious about what comes next.
